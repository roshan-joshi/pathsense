# PathSense Home — Story Bible

## Premise
A skilled person whose home has become harder to read. He is the main character; technology is the supporting character. The audience should remember a capable man seeking more choice and independence, not a magical camera.

## The person
- A **fictional, representative character**: a former café owner living in Australia who experienced acquired vision loss. He is inspired by real accessibility needs but is not a depiction of any individual. Every mode carries the note "Illustrative story. Character and scenes are fictional representations inspired by real accessibility needs."
- Uses today: a cane and training; a voice assistant for calls and messages; touch and his hands; memory of the place and years of kitchen knowledge.
- Everyday tasks in the story: finding the coffee; telling milk from juice; keys, the remote, a letter on the bench; fridge and pantry contents; which laundry bottle; simple daily routines.
- **Not added anywhere:** diagnosis, age, cause, family history, emotional condition, personal quotations.

## Tone
Respectful, warm, specific, unhurried. No pity, no "before: helpless / after: saved" framing. (Reference image 5's "Before PathSense" half was deliberately not used for this reason.) Technology is described with "designed to", "proposed", "could", "will test".

## Theme in one line
The challenge is not identifying an object; it is accomplishing the task that matters.

## Ten-question comprehension map
1. What is PathSense? → Ch1 tagline, Ch3 loop, reading mode "What PathSense is".
2. Whom is it for first? → Ch1.
3. What makes daily activities difficult? → Ch1 questions, Ch2 task list.
4. What would the AI attempt to do? → Ch3 (goal → view → candidate → describe → verify → confirm).
5. Why task completion over detection? → Ch2 statement and chain.
6. Why start with a smartphone? → Ch4 narration ("he already owns one"), stage 1 card.
7. What would the room camera add? → Ch4 stage 2 card and limits.
8. Why multiple cameras eventually? → Ch4 stage 3 card ("visible now vs last seen").
9. What will be tested? → Ch6 steps and six measures.
10. What partnership? → Ch7 four asks and next step.

## Character continuity
One character across all chapters: dark shirt, brown apron, striped tea towel, grey curly hair. Recurring objects: kettle, speckled cup, pantry jars, phone, white cane. All crops come from the seven supplied references; no other character images are introduced.

## The opening (v2)
Black screen → faint synthetic room tone (brown-noise bed, no recorded sound) → three italic cues ("a kettle", "a fridge door", "a room known by heart") → a warm ochre bloom and a single horizon line → the blurred kitchen sharpens into morning light → the person appears → the story begins. It is framed as emergence from the absence of *information*, never as "seeing again".

## Australian identity (v2)
Palette: deep charcoal, cream, sand, ochre, terracotta, red earth, muted gold, eucalyptus (verified states) and a touch of dusk blue. Images receive a warm grade. No Aboriginal motifs, patterns or symbols are reproduced; the inspiration is limited to colour and warmth, as the brief requires.
